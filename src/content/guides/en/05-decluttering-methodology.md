## The Physics of Clutter

Clutter is not just physical mass; it is postponed decisions. Every box in your attic and every overflowing drawer represents an unanswered question: *"What if I need this one day?"*, *"Should I feel guilty for getting rid of this gift?"*, or *"I paid good money for this."*

To clear a home permanently, we must replace emotional hesitation with a clear, reliable algorithm.

> "Have nothing in your house that you do not know to be useful or believe to be beautiful." — William Morris

## The 4-Box Sorting System

When attacking any room, closet, or cabinet, prepare four distinct containers:

1. **Keep (Active Use):** Items used regularly within the past 90 days or deeply cherished.
2. **Donate / Sell:** Functional items in good condition that you no longer use.
3. **Trash / Recycle:** Broken, expired, or non-repairable items.
4. **The Quarantine Box (Maybe):** Items you hesitate to discard.

### The Power of the Quarantine Box
Seal the "Maybe" box, write a date **6 months in the future** on top, and place it in the garage or closet. If you never open it during those 6 months, donate the entire box without looking inside.

## Conquering Sunk Cost Fallacy

The most common emotional trap is: *"I spent €200 on this coat, I can't throw it away."*
Remember: **The money is already gone.** Keeping an unused coat in your closet does not bring the €200 back — it only charges you a daily mental tax of guilt and cramped space.

## The Order of Attack
- **Start with low-emotion zones:** Bathroom toiletries, pantry goods, duplicate cables, old paperwork.
- **Move to medium zones:** Wardrobe, kitchen tools, hobby gear.
- **Save high-emotion zones for last:** Heirlooms, photo albums, children's art, sentimental gifts.
