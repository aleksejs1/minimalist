## Why Organizing Does Not Fix Excess

The multi-billion dollar home storage industry sells an alluring illusion: if you only buy more plastic bins, acrylic dividers, and labeled boxes, your home will finally stay clean.

In reality, **organizing is often just sophisticated hoarding.**

You cannot organize your way out of owning too much stuff. True order is created by reducing physical volume until storage becomes effortless.

> "The easiest way to organize your stuff is to get rid of most of it."

## The Core Laws of a Low-Maintenance Home

### 1. The Single Home Law
Every object you own must have exactly **one designated place** where it belongs. If an item does not have an immediate home, it floats on tables and counters, generating visual chaos.

### 2. The Clean Flat Surfaces Rule
Flat surfaces (dining tables, desks, kitchen counters) are visual magnets for clutter. Treat flat surfaces as **work areas, not storage areas**. Keep them 90% empty by default.

### 3. The One-In, One-Out Rule
To prevent passive accumulation, establish an unyielding boundary: whenever a new book, sweater, or coffee mug enters your home, an existing one must leave.

### 4. Visibility Over Stacking
Store items in a single layer where everything can be seen at a glance. Stacking items behind or beneath other items guarantees they will be forgotten and duplicated.

## The 10-Minute Evening Reset
Before going to sleep, spend 10 minutes returning all floating items to their single homes. Waking up to clear counters transforms your morning mindset.
