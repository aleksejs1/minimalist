## Why Decluttering Fails Without Values

You cannot subtract the non-essential until you have clearly defined what is essential. When people try to declutter without a clear understanding of their personal values, every item, invitation, and project feels equally "important."

The result is decision paralysis.

Minimalism begins not with a trash bag, but with an internal audit.

> "If it's not a definite YES, it's a clear NO." — Derek Sivers

## The Core Values Filter Exercise

### Step 1: The Value Brain Dump
Write down 20 to 30 words that resonate with you (e.g. *Autonomy, Health, Deep Connection, Financial Freedom, Creativity, Mastery, Calm, Nature*).

### Step 2: The Ruthless Cut
Force yourself to reduce that list down to **3 to 5 non-negotiable core values**. If everything is a priority, nothing is.

### Step 3: The 3-Filter Decision Test
Before agreeing to a project, purchasing an item, or accepting a social obligation, run it through your 3 core values:
1. *Does this directly support one of my top 3 priorities?*
2. *Does this require maintaining or managing something I don't care about?*
3. *Am I doing this out of genuine intention or social expectation?*

## Protecting the White Space

Your calendar and physical space will always expand to fill available capacity unless guarded by clear values. When you say "No" to the trivial many, you protect the energy required for the vital few.
